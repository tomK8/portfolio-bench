Today's holdings scored on two independent axes — how cheap it is, and how fast it is growing — from the same fundamentals cache the Quality tab uses. Nothing here is hand-maintained: the thresholds are checked in, every number scored against them is fetched.

§0 — How to read this report

Arm A is the strategy: buy £10k of a name when it closes at the bottom decile of its trailing one-year range (having first spent ten sessions above that floor), sell at +10%. Arm B buys the same £10k on the day of Arm A's first signal and simply holds. Arm C makes the same number of £10k purchases on random dates in the same window and holds — repeated 1,000 times, giving a distribution; Arm A's bootstrap percentile says where the strategy lands in that distribution (50 = indistinguishable from random timing; cells inside the 25–75 band are dimmed). Arm D buys at the top decile instead — if D beats A, the cohort trends and doesn't mean-revert. Censored trades are positions still open when the data ends: counted and marked to market, never pretended to be wins or losses. Max adverse excursion is the worst unrealised loss a trade sat through before exiting — what "hold until it bounces" actually required. Two capital bases are shown because they answer different questions: return on allocated capital counts idle-cash drag against the strategy; return per pound-year deployed measures timing skill only while money is in the market. The verdict line requires Arm A to beat both Arm B and Arm C's median — otherwise it says so in plain words.

Your holdings

In-specie transfers — the same shares moving from one account to another, with nothing sold, nothing bought and no cash moving anywhere. No broker export announces this: a cash statement cannot (no cash moves, so no row appears in it), and the holdings files show it only after the fact, as a position that has vanished from one export and turned up in another. So the cash ledger goes on reporting the position at the old account indefinitely — right about what you hold, wrong about where. This is the missing input.

The cost basis moves with the shares, and it is read off the ledger rather than typed: the amount written on each leg is the source account's own surviving FIFO cost, so your total cost — and therefore your gain — is exactly what it was before. Using market value instead would rewrite the cost basis and destroy the gain. Two rows are written, one per account, in a single transaction; neither touches cash. The whole position moves — partial transfers are not offered.

Record it the day you initiate it, not when it lands. The ledger then says the destination straight away, and until the shares actually arrive the Reconcile tab reports the position as present in the ledger and missing from the destination's export — which is exactly the signal that it is still in flight.

Compare the portfolio on any two dates. Both ends are valued from the cash ledger — positions replayed from every trade and marked at that date's stored daily close, cash converted at that date's historical FX — so the two ends sit on one consistent basis instead of whatever the brokers happened to report on the days you synced. Same engine as Value over time and Returns.

Three numbers answer "how did it do", and they only agree when no money moved: Change is the raw end − start, external money included. Gain takes that money back out — the market's own contribution. Total return is the chain-linked daily time-weighted return, which is flow-neutral and so the one to hold against an index; over the full history it reproduces the Returns tab's since-inception figure. Annualised is blank under 30 days — compounding a two-week move 26× is noise. Per-symbol P&L for the same window is on the Attribution tab.

External money is split in two because it is two different things. Contributions is money paid in, from the ledger's contribution rows; an internal AJ Bell → II SIPP transfer nets to zero across them, since the outbound leg is recorded on the AJ Bell side. Brought forward (shown only when it applies) is an account's opening balance arriving in the ledger — the Roth IRA's history starts mid-way, so its opening balance lands on one day as a step. It is not money paid in, but it does have to come off the gain, or the whole opening balance would read as a one-day profit.

Per-symbol GBP P&L over a date window: end_value − start_value + window_cash_flows, where cash flows sum every ledger row for that symbol (buys negative, sells positive, dividends positive, FX baked in at trade date). Captures realised + unrealised gains plus dividends. Trades at fair value contribute zero — only subsequent market moves and dividends do. Bonds use the per-£100 nominal formula. A symbol whose value can't be established on a boundary (no price_history rows, or no FX rate for its currency) is excluded from the table and from every total, and listed in the warning above — valuing it at zero instead would quietly overstate the P&L by its whole position. End-value uses live intraday + live FX when the window ends today, daily close + historical FX otherwise. % of total is each row's share of the net P&L, so the column sums to 100% — but when winners and losers nearly cancel, the denominator is small and individual shares inflate past 100%. Return % = P&L ÷ peak GBP capital deployed in the window; Annualised % grosses that up to a 365-day year (so a 2% move over a month reads as ~27%). It is blank under 30 days: compounding a 5-day move 73× produces five-figure percentages that mean nothing.

Day Δ compares against the last regular close before that quote's own trading session — so a US name still shows yesterday's full session move at 09:00 London, and a pre-market quote is measured against last night's close. Quote cadence depends on Yahoo (free 1-min intraday, refetched every minute) and market hours: .L names freeze at the 16:30 BST close, US names tick from 09:00 BST pre-market through 05:00 BST after-hours. Day Δ is GBP-denominated (price move + FX move on the position). Click a row for the price chart, momentum, vol term structure and fundamentals.
