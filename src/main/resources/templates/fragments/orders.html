Highlighted rows sit on the wrong side of their own limit — a buy whose limit is at or above the market, or a sell at or below it. A resting order in that state should already have executed, so either the Current price is stale (nothing keeps an order-only symbol's feed warm between ticks) or the broker export pre-dates the fill.

Rows marked pre-market 13:58 / after-hours 21:40 are priced off an extended-hours quote — used because it's the newest price available, and the alternative is a close that's hours older. The time is the print time, and it's always shown: these sessions trade thinly, so a quote can sit unchanged for a quarter of an hour and still be the latest one Yahoo has. Those rows never raise the amber through-limit flag either — a resting regular-hours order doesn't fill outside regular hours, so sitting through the limit on a thin pre-market print isn't the same signal.

P(N) is the same touch estimate over a horizon you pick. Every row starts on 90 days — a common horizon is what makes the column comparable across orders, the way P(5d) and P(20d) already are — and each row's box is editable, so an order's own days-to-expiry is a keystroke away when that is the question. Days become trading sessions, since σ scales by √sessions: 90 calendar days is about 64 sessions of diffusion, not 90. Read the whole column as indicative rather than calibrated — the model assumes no drift, which is a fair convention over a week and an assumption over a quarter; push a row much past six months and the figure is marked to say so.

Click any row for the price popup — chart, momentum, vol term structure and fundamentals, the same one the Live and Watchlist tables open. Rows whose instrument isn't mapped to a ticker yet aren't clickable; map them above and they will be.
